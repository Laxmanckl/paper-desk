# Central-Bank Reader: memo (2026-10-10)

## Where the two central banks stand

**Fed.** On 16 Sep 2026 the FOMC hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00%. The vote was 12-0 and all 19 participants supported it. This was the first hike since 2023. Chair Warsh called it "removing accommodation" and said the "predominant focus is on the price stability side". The median 2026 dot is 4.125%, which implies one more hike this year. The minutes (released 7 Oct) say another hike "would likely be appropriate by year end". The cited inflation pressures are energy, AI spending and past tariffs, with staff estimates of August PCE at 3.8% and core at 3.4%. The minutes barely moved markets. That day DXY was about 102.3 and the US 10-year touched 5.36%, the highest since 2002.

Expectations have swung a lot. CME odds of an October hike were about 77% on 23 Sep. They fell to about 20% after weak September payrolls and downward revisions, and stood at about 18% (82% no change) by 9 Oct. December hike odds are about 70-81%. The next decision is **28 Oct**. MUFG's base case is a skip in October and a hike in December.

**ECB.** On 10 Sep 2026 the ECB hiked 25bp, its second hike this year, taking the deposit rate to 2.50%. Lagarde called it a "no-brainer" after oil went back above $100 following the US-Iran attacks. On 29 Sep she pushed back on bets for faster hikes: the energy shock is "too large to look through", but she wants a "measured" response because there is no wage pass-through yet and higher long yields are already tightening conditions. EUR/USD fell to about 1.1350 on her remarks.

October hike odds were about 60% on 24 Sep. Standard Chartered now sees them as limited: core inflation is 2.5%, and it expects the ECB to wait for the December projections. Nagel says mildly restrictive policy is possible. The next decision is **29 Oct, one day after the Fed**. French fiscal worries are also weighing on the euro, which recently hit a 17-month low near 1.116.

**Central-bank gold buying.** Q2 2026 was a record 289t (WGC), led by Poland (+51t) and China (+33t). Q1 was weak at 57t. The PBoC added 21t in September, its 23rd straight month of buying and its largest month since Sep 2023 (released 7 Oct). It bought into the September price dip. This is a structural bid under gold, but it is slow and only reported monthly. It is not a timing signal I can turn into fixed rules with a decent sample: the current PBoC streak gives about 23 events. So I use it only as context. It supports a "buy weakness" bias in gold rather than shorting breakdowns.

**Read-through for the desk.** Gold (-5.7% over 20 days, 5% below its 50-day average) and EUR/USD (-3.7% over 20 days, at the bottom of its 20-day range) have both been pushed by a hawkish Fed repricing and a firm dollar. Both now sit just ahead of a back-to-back Fed/ECB pair on 28-29 Oct.

The only rule-based edge I'm willing to propose comes from the scheduled meeting dates themselves. I'm sceptical of both ideas below:
- Event-day samples are small: about 8 FOMC and 8 ECB meetings a year.
- Effects documented in the literature, like the pre-FOMC drift, may have been arbitraged away.
- This October the two meetings are one day apart, so an ECB-day move partly reflects the Fed.

Both ideas therefore need a non-event baseline to beat, not just a positive expectancy.

## Ideas

1. **Gold post-FOMC rebound after a pre-meeting sell-off (daily).** Gold tends to be sold ahead of a hawkish-feared FOMC and to recover once the decision removes the uncertainty. In July 2026, gold fell toward $4,000 into the meeting and rebounded above $4,100 after the hold (ATFX). Central-bank dip buying adds a floor. The rules are fixed and use only scheduled dates.
2. **EUR/USD ECB-day follow-through (daily).** A decisive ECB-day close carries information about the policy path, such as Lagarde's "measured" pushback, and FX often keeps drifting for days as rate expectations reprice. The test is whether a strong, well-located ECB-day bar predicts the next week.

I'm deliberately not proposing a third idea. A PBoC-release-day rule (the 7th of each month) has too few events. A 1-minute "fade the FOMC spike" scalp would hit slippage and spread blowouts at 18:00 UTC that our cost cap (at most 25% of risk) would rarely pass.
