# Skeptic memo: H4-H9

**Overall:** none of these are scalps, so the cost problem that is plausibly killing H2/H3 mostly goes away. A daily ATR on gold or EUR/USD is huge compared with the spread. The real dangers here are **small samples, clustered signals and rules built to fit the story**. H7, H8 and H9 are three versions of one bet, "event days carry information". Count them as one family. If you test the follow-through and fade versions of all three, at least one will look good by luck. Freeze every parameter before the first run. No tuning.

**H4: post-shock box breakout (XAUUSD).** Days of 3x ATR(20) on gold are rare. Requiring a tight 5-close box afterwards, plus a breakout within 15 days, leaves maybe 1-3 setups a year. The ">=30 setups" bar may never be reached honestly. There are also two variants, which halves each sample. The shock day also inflates ATR(20), so "2.5 ATR" is a loose box right after the shock and a tighter one as ATR decays. The box rule therefore drifts on its own. There are seven free knobs (3x, 5, 2.5, 0.25, 2-ATR cap, 2R, 15d). With about 30 trades, any result is noise. **Test**, but only because it's cheap. Run it once on frozen parameters, and if it comes out under 30 setups, call the result "unknown", not "promising".

**H5: EUR/USD over-extension snap-back.** The setup only appears in big dollar trends (2008, 2014-15, 2022). Those are exactly the regimes where buying stretched EUR/USD gets run over. Signals cluster: "25 setups" is probably 6-10 independent episodes, so the effective sample is tiny. The exits are lopsided. The stop sits below the stretch low, but the target is the *nearer* of EMA20 or 2R, and in a stretch EMA20 is often close. The realised reward:risk is likely well under 1, so you need a high hit rate that counter-trend entries rarely deliver. **Test**, with a hard rule: group signals by episode and report results per episode, not per trade.

**H6: gold deep dip plus EUR/USD 5-day high.** This rule was written to fit today's chart (the "as now" in the brief gives it away). It has three conditions at once. Gold closing >=4% under SMA50 is uncommon in a market that mostly trended up. It must also be within 1 ATR of its 20-day low, and EUR/USD must make a 5-day closing high on the same bar. My guess is under 15 signals in 10 years, so it fails its own sample test by construction. The gold/dollar link it relies on broke in 2022-24, when both rallied together. Any gold dip-buy since 2019 looks good just from the bull-market drift, so a "win" would prove nothing about the dollar filter. **Drop.** If you want the idea, test plain "gold >=4% below SMA50 near the 20d low" as a dip-buy with a random-entry control. Treat the EUR/USD filter as a later add-on, not the core.

**H7: CPI/NFP-day follow-through or fade.** By the next open, the surprise is priced in. Daily moves of >=1 ATR on NFP or CPI days are common and mostly reflect the noise of the release, not a lasting surprise. Testing both directions means two shots at the same coin flip. NFP falls on a Friday, so "next open" is the Sunday reopen, with weekend gap risk inside a tight 1.0-ATR stop. The Fed-pricing story ("20% October hike odds") plays no part in the rule, so ignore it. Even so, this is the cleanest idea here. Event dates are public (BLS archive), it has the most trades (100+), and its falsification test includes a split into two halves. **Test.** Require the same sign in both halves and over both markets.

**H8: gold post-FOMC rebound.** Pre- and post-FOMC drift in gold is widely published, so any edge has probably been arbitraged away. The 2008-2013 QE meetings will dominate the result and will not repeat. About 40-60 trades over 18 years spans several Fed regimes. The 2019-2026 gold bull run flatters any long-only rule. The design fixes most of this, though: it has a control on non-FOMC days with the same 1% drop, and a check on the 2020-2026 subsample. **Test.** Treat the control comparison as the only result that matters, not raw expectancy.

**H9: EUR/USD ECB-day follow-through.** The daily bar on an ECB day is not a pure ECB reaction. ECB Thursdays share the day with US jobless claims and sometimes CPI or retail sales. The 0.6-ATR move and the close-location filter will pick up US data as often as Lagarde. Expect roughly 40-50 qualifying meetings since 2010, dominated by the 2014-15 QE era. The evidence for a post-ECB drift is weak, and 7 days is long enough for the next US print to swamp it. Structurally this is H7 again. **Test**, but at low priority. Also exclude ECB days that share the day with a tier-1 US release, not just the ones next to an FOMC decision. Otherwise you can't separate an ECB effect from a US one.
