# Macro Watcher memo, 2026-10-10

## Where macro stands
- **Fed is hiking again.** It raised rates 25bp on 16 Sep to 3.75–4.00%, its first hike since 2023. 16 of 18 officials see at least one more hike by December. Markets put roughly 20% odds on an October hike and 70–80% on a hike by December. The September FOMC minutes (out 7 Oct) were hawkish. ([political.org](https://political.org/2026/10/07/dollar-holds-near-18-month-high-as-hawkish-fed-minutes-reinforce-inflation-warnings/), [Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/us-cpi-preview-september-2026-forecasts-and-the-core-cpi-outlook))
- **Yields are near multi-decade highs.** The US 10-year is around 5.2–5.3%, with a touch of about 5.35% that reports call the highest since 2002. The 30-year is around 5.6%. ([USAGOLD 7 Oct](https://www.usagold.com/daily-precious-metals-market-report-october-7-2026/), [political.org](https://political.org/2026/10/07/dollar-holds-near-18-month-high-as-hawkish-fed-minutes-reinforce-inflation-warnings/))
- **Inflation is an energy shock, not a core problem.** Fighting in the Middle East and Hormuz disruption are pushing energy prices up. US headline CPI was 3.4% in August, and September is expected at 3.6–3.7% with core flat near 2.4%. Euro-area September flash HICP was 3.8% (energy +18.8%), with core at 2.5%. ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/us-cpi-preview-september-2026-forecasts-and-the-core-cpi-outlook), [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/euro-area/inflation-cpi))
- **US jobs are weak.** September payrolls rose 29k against about 90k expected. Unemployment rose to 4.2%. Wages grew 3.0% y/y, the lowest since 2021. Payrolls for the two prior months were revised down by 60k. On release, EUR/USD rose about 0.4% and the 10-year fell 6bp, but both moves had fully reversed within days. ([BabyPips](https://www.babypips.com/news/headline-us-jobs-report-september-2026-payrolls-miss-fed-hike-odds), [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/daily-brief/2026-10-02))
- **ECB is hiking too.** It raised the deposit rate to 2.50% (effective 16 Sep) with no committed path. Prediction markets put about 94% on a hold at the 29 Oct meeting. ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40070896), [Robinhood/Kalshi](https://invest.robinhood.com/us/en/prediction-markets/economics/events/european-central-bank-rate-decision-in-october-oct-29-2026/))
- **The dollar is strong.** DXY is about 102.2–102.5, near an 18-month high. US yields still dominate, even though the ECB is hiking too.
- **Gold is under rate pressure.** It fell more than 8% in September, yet gold ETFs took in more than 70t. The World Gold Council (cited by USAGOLD) blames liquidation of leveraged COMEX positions, not physical selling. That means the drop came from positioning while the physical bid held. ([USAGOLD](https://www.usagold.com/daily-precious-metals-market-report-october-7-2026/))
- **Risk mood is mixed.** The S&P 500 had eight record closes in a row before a small pullback. This is a rates-driven move, not a flight from risk. ([heygotrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/fed-rate-cuts-priced-out-2026-treasury-yields-hammack-jolts/))

## Reasoning
Both of our markets are falling for one reason: US real yields and the dollar. Gold is -5.7% over 20 days and about 5% below its 50-day average. EUR/USD is at a fresh 20-day closing low of 1.1201.

The question I can test is how macro shocks pass into price. I see two possible edges:

1. **Gold mean-reversion after a positioning flush.** When gold is stretched far below trend from rate pressure, the dollar easing (EUR/USD turning up) should mark the point where the yield pressure eases. Gold alone can bounce on noise. A dollar confirmation from the other market is the macro filter.
2. **Follow-through after big US data days.** Release-day moves in a hiking cycle either stick or get faded. The September NFP was a clear example of the fade, so I want to test whether follow-through or fade is the rule, using a fixed event calendar.

**Caveats:**
- Idea 2 needs a historical list of US CPI and NFP release dates, which is public from BLS. Without it, idea 2 cannot be tested.
- Samples are small: about 24 events a year, and only a fraction pass the size filter.
- Idea 1 is not the same as H1. H1 trades with the trend. Idea 1 is counter-trend and needs a signal from the other market.

I am not proposing a CPI-minute scalp. H3's 13 straight losses show that breakout rules around fixed times have struggled here, and spreads at the release are wide.

## Ideas
1. **Gold stretched-low reversal confirmed by a softer dollar (daily, XAUUSD).**
   - **Rule:** Buy XAUUSD at the next daily open when all three are true on the same completed bar: gold closes at least 4% below its SMA50; gold is within 1 ATR(14) of its 20-day low close; EUR/USD closes at a 5-day closing high.
   - **Exits:** Stop 1.5 ATR, target 2.5 ATR, max hold 15 days.
2. **Follow-through on big US data days (daily, EURUSD and XAUUSD).**
   - **Rule:** On a US CPI or NFP release day, if the daily bar's absolute close-to-close move is at least 1.0 ATR(14), enter at the next open in the direction of that day's close.
   - **Exits:** Stop 1.0 ATR, target 1.5 ATR, max hold 5 days.
   - **Fade variant:** Test the reverse-direction version with the same rules alongside, so the result says which one holds.
