# Price Analyst · 2026-10-12

## Price Analyst memo

Both markets are choppy: gold -3.7% under its 50-day average after a 4% pullback, EUR/USD -2.6% under its average after a steady 3.4% slide. The ledger says indicator settings on their own (RSI, MA crosses, ML on indicators) have no edge here, and that 1-minute trading dies on spreads. So I only propose ideas where a *forced or habitual flow* explains the pattern, on 1-hour bars or slower.

**1. Weekend gap, partial fill (1-hour bars, all 9 markets).**
FX and gold close Friday ~21:00 UTC and reopen Sunday ~21:00–22:00 UTC. The first hour trades in the thinnest liquidity of the week (Wellington/Sydney only): a few dealers quote wide and reprice weekend news in one jump. When Tokyo and then London arrive, real two-way flow returns and part of an over-sized jump is taken back. *Who loses:* stop orders and weekend-news reactions filled in the thin first hour. *Why not traded away:* the size is small and only once a week per market; big desks don't bother.
Rule: at the close of the week's first 1-hour bar, if its close is more than 0.5 ATR(14, 1-hour) away from Friday's last close, trade back toward Friday's close. Target: half the gap. Stop: 1.5x the gap beyond the entry. Out after 24 bars.
Chance for that shape: stop 1.5 / (1.5 + 0.5) = 75%. I expect ~85%. Proven wrong if wins are within 2 standard errors of 75%, or it loses after double spreads (the first hour's spread is the widest of the week, which is the strongest argument against it).

**2. Gold's Asia-hours drift (1-hour bars, XAUUSD only).**
Gold's return has been concentrated in Asian hours and given back in London/NY hours (repeatedly documented; physical buying in Shanghai/India during Asia, futures and fixing-related selling in the West). Rule: buy at the 23:00 UTC bar close, exit at the 07:00 UTC bar close (8 bars). Symmetric 2 ATR stop and target so the time exit decides nearly every trade. Chance: 50%. I expect ~55% with winners slightly larger than losers. It cannot reach 85%; I list it because it is a real, documented effect. Proven wrong if the 2020–2025 4-hour/1-hour history shows no positive Asia-hours return after costs.
